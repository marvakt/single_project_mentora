MENTAL HEALTH SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION GUIDELINES

This document provides evidence-based guidelines for classifying mental health symptom severity levels. These guidelines are based on DSM-5 criteria, PHQ-9 scoring, and clinical best practices.

================================================================================
SEVERITY LEVELS OVERVIEW
================================================================================

MILD SEVERITY
- Symptoms present but manageable
- Minimal impact on daily functioning
- Can maintain work, school, and social activities
- May experience occasional distress
- Responds well to self-care and counseling

MODERATE SEVERITY
- Noticeable symptoms affecting quality of life
- Some difficulty with daily functioning
- Work/school performance may be impaired
- Social withdrawal or relationship strain
- Requires professional therapeutic intervention

SEVERE SEVERITY
- Significant symptoms causing major distress
- Substantial impairment in daily functioning
- Unable to maintain normal work/school/social activities
- May include crisis symptoms or safety concerns
- Requires immediate professional intervention, possibly medication

================================================================================
DEPRESSION SEVERITY INDICATORS
================================================================================

MILD DEPRESSION:
- Depressed mood on some days
- Reduced interest in 1-2 activities
- Sleep disturbances (difficulty falling asleep or occasional oversleeping)
- Mild fatigue or low energy
- Can still engage in most activities with effort
- Negative thoughts present but not overwhelming
- Duration: 2+ weeks of symptoms

MODERATE DEPRESSION:
- Depressed mood most days
- Loss of interest in multiple previously enjoyed activities (anhedonia)
- Significant sleep problems (insomnia or hypersomnia most nights)
- Persistent fatigue and low energy
- Concentration difficulties affecting work/school
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Appetite changes (significant weight loss or gain)
- Psychomotor changes (restlessness or slowing)
- Duration: 2+ weeks with consistent symptoms

SEVERE DEPRESSION:
- Persistent depressed mood nearly every day
- Complete loss of interest in almost all activities
- Severe sleep disturbances (chronic insomnia or excessive sleeping)
- Extreme fatigue, inability to complete basic tasks
- Severe concentration problems, indecisiveness
- Strong feelings of worthlessness or inappropriate guilt
- Significant appetite/weight changes
- Thoughts of death or suicidal ideation
- Inability to function in work, school, or relationships
- Duration: 2+ weeks with severe, persistent symptoms

================================================================================
ANXIETY SEVERITY INDICATORS
================================================================================

MILD ANXIETY:
- Occasional worry or nervousness
- Mild physical symptoms (slight tension, occasional rapid heartbeat)
- Can manage symptoms with relaxation techniques
- Anxiety doesn't significantly interfere with activities
- Able to redirect worried thoughts
- Duration: Symptoms present but manageable

MODERATE ANXIETY:
- Frequent worry that's difficult to control
- Multiple physical symptoms (muscle tension, restlessness, fatigue)
- Difficulty concentrating due to worry
- Sleep disturbances from anxiety
- Avoidance of some situations
- Noticeable impact on work, school, or social life
- Duration: Symptoms present more days than not for 6+ months (GAD) or persistent in specific situations

SEVERE ANXIETY:
- Constant, overwhelming worry or fear
- Intense physical symptoms (panic attacks, chest pain, dizziness, nausea)
- Severe avoidance behaviors
- Inability to function normally
- Significant impairment in multiple life areas
- May include panic disorder, severe social anxiety, or agoraphobia
- Duration: Persistent and debilitating symptoms

================================================================================
COMBINED SYMPTOM PATTERNS
================================================================================

MILD SEVERITY PATTERN:
- 2-4 symptoms present
- Symptoms occur occasionally or intermittently
- Minimal functional impairment
- Can still work, study, and maintain relationships
- Responds to lifestyle changes and self-help strategies
- PHQ-9 Score: 5-9

MODERATE SEVERITY PATTERN:
- 5-7 symptoms present
- Symptoms occur frequently (more than half the days)
- Moderate functional impairment
- Difficulty maintaining normal routines
- Requires professional therapy (counseling or psychology)
- May benefit from structured interventions (CBT, DBT)
- PHQ-9 Score: 10-14

MODERATELY SEVERE PATTERN:
- 7-9 symptoms present
- Symptoms occur most days
- Significant functional impairment
- Struggling to maintain work, school, or relationships
- Requires intensive therapy
- May need medication evaluation
- PHQ-9 Score: 15-19

SEVERE PATTERN:
- 8-10 symptoms present
- Symptoms occur nearly every day
- Severe functional impairment or inability to function
- May include suicidal thoughts or self-harm ideation
- Requires immediate psychiatric intervention
- Medication management likely needed
- Crisis support may be necessary
- PHQ-9 Score: 20-27

================================================================================
DURATION AND FREQUENCY CRITERIA
================================================================================

ACUTE (Short-term):
- Symptoms present for 2 weeks to 3 months
- May be related to specific stressor or life event
- Often responds well to brief intervention

PERSISTENT (Chronic):
- Symptoms present for 3+ months
- May indicate chronic condition requiring ongoing treatment
- Needs comprehensive treatment plan

RECURRENT:
- History of previous episodes
- Current episode represents relapse
- May need maintenance treatment to prevent future episodes

================================================================================
FUNCTIONAL IMPAIRMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

MINIMAL IMPAIRMENT (Mild):
- Can perform job/school duties with some extra effort
- Maintains most relationships
- Self-care intact
- Participates in some social activities

MODERATE IMPAIRMENT (Moderate):
- Reduced productivity at work/school
- Some relationship strain
- Self-care sometimes neglected
- Limited social engagement

SEVERE IMPAIRMENT (Severe):
- Unable to work or attend school
- Relationship breakdown or isolation
- Self-care significantly compromised
- Complete social withdrawal

CRITICAL IMPAIRMENT (Severe/Crisis):
- Cannot perform basic daily activities
- Complete isolation
- Self-care absent
- Safety concerns present

================================================================================
RISK FACTORS REQUIRING SEVERITY ESCALATION
================================================================================

IMMEDIATE ESCALATION TO SEVERE:
- Suicidal ideation with plan or intent
- Self-harm behaviors
- Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions)
- Severe substance abuse complicating mental health
- Inability to care for self (hygiene, eating, safety)
- Homicidal thoughts
- Severe agitation or aggression

ESCALATION TO MODERATE-SEVERE:
- Passive suicidal thoughts without plan
- History of self-harm
- Significant substance use
- Rapid deterioration in functioning
- Multiple previous episodes
- Poor response to previous treatment
- Limited social support

================================================================================
SPECIALIST ROUTING GUIDELINES
================================================================================

COUNSELOR (Mild Severity):
- Adjustment issues and life transitions
- Mild stress, anxiety, or depression
- Relationship or family issues
- Grief and loss (uncomplicated)
- Self-esteem and personal growth
- Preventive mental health care

PSYCHOLOGIST (Moderate Severity):
- Moderate depression or anxiety
- Trauma and PTSD
- Eating disorders
- OCD and related disorders
- Personality disorders
- Complex grief
- Requires evidence-based therapy (CBT, DBT, EMDR)
- Psychological testing and assessment

PSYCHIATRIST (Severe Severity):
- Severe depression or anxiety
- Bipolar disorder
- Schizophrenia and psychotic disorders
- Treatment-resistant conditions
- Medication management needed
- Suicidal ideation or self-harm risk
- Hospitalization consideration
- Complex cases requiring medical oversight

================================================================================
SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
================================================================================

COMORBIDITY:
- Multiple conditions increase severity
- Anxiety + Depression = Higher severity classification
- Substance use + Mental health = Requires specialized treatment

TRAUMA HISTORY:
- PTSD or trauma may elevate severity
- Requires trauma-informed care approach

AGE FACTORS:
- Adolescents: Consider developmental context
- Elderly: Consider medical comorbidities and isolation

CULTURAL FACTORS:
- Cultural expression of symptoms varies
- Consider cultural context in assessment
- Language barriers may affect symptom reporting

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS:
- Homelessness, poverty, or lack of support increases severity
- Access to care affects treatment planning

================================================================================
CRISIS INDICATORS (IMMEDIATE INTERVENTION REQUIRED)
================================================================================

RED FLAGS:
- Active suicidal ideation with plan
- Recent suicide attempt
- Self-harm with increasing frequency
- Psychotic symptoms
- Severe agitation or inability to ensure safety
- Acute substance intoxication affecting judgment
- Homicidal ideation

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
- Contact emergency services (911)
- Crisis hotline: 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline)
- Emergency room evaluation
- Mobile crisis team
- Do not leave person alone

================================================================================
NOTES ON ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

This document provides guidelines for educational purposes and to support clinical decision-making tools. It should NOT replace professional clinical judgment. All severity assessments should be:

1. Individualized to the person's unique situation
2. Culturally sensitive and contextually appropriate
3. Reviewed by qualified mental health professionals
4. Updated regularly as symptoms change
5. Integrated with comprehensive clinical assessment

Severity classification is a starting point for treatment planning, not a definitive diagnosis. Always err on the side of caution and escalate care when uncertain.

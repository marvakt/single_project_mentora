EVIDENCE-BASED COPING STRATEGIES AND MENTAL HEALTH TECHNIQUES

This document contains practical, evidence-based coping strategies and self-care techniques for managing mental health symptoms. These strategies are organized by symptom type and severity level.

================================================================================
BREATHING EXERCISES
================================================================================

4-7-8 BREATHING TECHNIQUE:
Purpose: Reduces anxiety, promotes relaxation, helps with sleep
How to Practice:
1. Sit or lie in a comfortable position
2. Place tongue behind upper front teeth
3. Exhale completely through mouth (whoosh sound)
4. Close mouth, inhale through nose for 4 counts
5. Hold breath for 7 counts
6. Exhale through mouth for 8 counts (whoosh sound)
7. Repeat cycle 3-4 times

Best For: Anxiety, panic attacks, insomnia, stress
Frequency: 2-3 times daily, or as needed during stress
Evidence: Activates parasympathetic nervous system, reduces cortisol

BOX BREATHING (4-4-4-4):
Purpose: Calms nervous system, improves focus, reduces stress
How to Practice:
1. Sit upright with feet flat on floor
2. Inhale through nose for 4 counts
3. Hold breath for 4 counts
4. Exhale through mouth for 4 counts
5. Hold empty for 4 counts
6. Repeat for 5-10 cycles

Best For: Anxiety, stress, concentration issues, panic
Frequency: Multiple times daily, especially before stressful situations
Evidence: Used by Navy SEALs, proven to reduce stress response

DIAPHRAGMATIC BREATHING:
Purpose: Deep relaxation, reduces physical tension
How to Practice:
1. Lie on back or sit comfortably
2. Place one hand on chest, one on belly
3. Breathe in through nose, expanding belly (not chest)
4. Exhale slowly through pursed lips
5. Belly should rise and fall, chest stays relatively still
6. Practice for 5-10 minutes

Best For: General anxiety, physical tension, stress
Frequency: Daily practice, 5-10 minutes
Evidence: Reduces heart rate and blood pressure

ALTERNATE NOSTRIL BREATHING:
Purpose: Balances nervous system, reduces anxiety
How to Practice:
1. Sit comfortably with straight spine
2. Use right thumb to close right nostril
3. Inhale through left nostril
4. Close left nostril with ring finger, release right
5. Exhale through right nostril
6. Inhale through right nostril
7. Switch and exhale through left
8. Continue for 5-10 rounds

Best For: Anxiety, racing thoughts, sleep preparation
Frequency: Once or twice daily
Evidence: Balances sympathetic and parasympathetic systems

================================================================================
GROUNDING TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

5-4-3-2-1 TECHNIQUE:
Purpose: Anchors to present moment, reduces anxiety and dissociation
How to Practice:
1. Acknowledge 5 things you can SEE (look around, name them)
2. Acknowledge 4 things you can TOUCH (feel textures)
3. Acknowledge 3 things you can HEAR (listen carefully)
4. Acknowledge 2 things you can SMELL (or favorite scents)
5. Acknowledge 1 thing you can TASTE (or something you like to taste)

Best For: Panic attacks, anxiety, dissociation, overwhelming emotions
When to Use: During acute anxiety or when feeling disconnected
Evidence: Engages all senses to ground in present reality

PHYSICAL GROUNDING:
Purpose: Reconnects with body and present moment
Techniques:
- Stomp feet on ground, notice the sensation
- Hold ice cube in hand, focus on temperature
- Splash cold water on face
- Press hands together firmly
- Do wall push-ups, notice muscle engagement
- Stretch body, notice each muscle

Best For: Panic, dissociation, flashbacks, intense emotions
When to Use: Immediate relief during crisis moments

MENTAL GROUNDING:
Purpose: Redirects anxious thoughts
Techniques:
- Count backwards from 100 by 7s
- Name all objects in room of specific color
- Recite multiplication tables
- List countries, cities, or animals alphabetically
- Describe surroundings in detail
- Play categories game (name 5 fruits, 5 cars, etc.)

Best For: Racing thoughts, anxiety, intrusive thoughts
When to Use: When mind is overwhelmed or spiraling

EMOTIONAL GROUNDING:
Purpose: Validates and processes emotions safely
Techniques:
- Name the emotion you're feeling
- Rate emotion intensity 1-10
- Remind yourself: "This feeling is temporary"
- List 3 things you're grateful for
- Think of favorite place, describe it in detail
- Remember a time you felt calm and safe

Best For: Emotional overwhelm, mood swings
When to Use: During intense emotional experiences

SLEEP HYGIENE PRACTICES
================================================================================

BEDTIME ROUTINE:
- Consistent sleep schedule (same bedtime/wake time daily)
- 30-60 minute wind-down routine before bed
- Dim lights 1 hour before sleep
- Avoid screens 30-60 minutes before bed
- Read, journal, or light stretching
- Keep bedroom cool (65-68°F / 18-20°C)

SLEEP ENVIRONMENT:
- Dark room (blackout curtains or eye mask)
- Quiet (white noise machine if needed)
- Comfortable mattress and pillows
- Reserve bed for sleep only (not work or TV)
- Remove clocks from view

DAYTIME HABITS FOR BETTER SLEEP:
- Morning sunlight exposure (15-30 minutes)
- Regular exercise (but not within 3 hours of bedtime)
- Limit caffeine after 2 PM
- Avoid large meals before bed
- Limit daytime naps to 20-30 minutes before 3 PM

WHEN YOU CAN'T SLEEP:
- Don't lie awake for more than 20 minutes
- Get up, do quiet activity in dim light
- Return to bed when sleepy
- Avoid checking time repeatedly
- Practice 4-7-8 breathing or progressive muscle relaxation

Best For: Insomnia, anxiety-related sleep issues, depression
Evidence: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) proven effective

================================================================================
COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING BASICS
================================================================================

IDENTIFYING NEGATIVE THOUGHT PATTERNS:
Common Distortions:
- All-or-nothing thinking: "I failed once, I'm a complete failure"
- Catastrophizing: "This will be a disaster"
- Mind reading: "They think I'm stupid"
- Overgeneralization: "I always mess up"
- Emotional reasoning: "I feel worthless, so I must be worthless"

CHALLENGING NEGATIVE THOUGHTS:
Questions to Ask:
1. What's the evidence for this thought?
2. What's the evidence against it?
3. Is there an alternative explanation?
4. What would I tell a friend thinking this?
5. What's the worst that could happen? Best? Most realistic?
6. Will this matter in 5 years?

REFRAMING TECHNIQUE:
Negative: "I can't do anything right"
Reframe: "I'm struggling with this task, but I've succeeded before and can learn"

Negative: "Everyone hates me"
Reframe: "Some people may not like me, but I have friends who care"

Best For: Depression, anxiety, low self-esteem
Evidence: Core component of CBT, highly effective

================================================================================
PHYSICAL ACTIVITY RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

FOR DEPRESSION:
- 30 minutes moderate exercise, 5 days/week
- Walking, jogging, cycling, swimming
- Exercise releases endorphins (natural mood boosters)
- Outdoor exercise provides additional benefits (sunlight, nature)
- Start small: even 10 minutes helps

FOR ANXIETY:
- Rhythmic, aerobic exercise (walking, running, cycling)
- Yoga or tai chi for mind-body connection
- Exercise burns stress hormones (cortisol, adrenaline)
- Group classes provide social connection

GENTLE OPTIONS:
- Walking in nature
- Gentle yoga or stretching
- Tai chi or qigong
- Swimming or water aerobics
- Dancing to favorite music

TIPS FOR STARTING:
- Start with 5-10 minutes if needed
- Choose activities you enjoy
- Exercise with friend for accountability
- Schedule it like an appointment
- Track progress for motivation

Evidence: Exercise as effective as medication for mild-moderate depression

MINDFULNESS AND MEDITATION
================================================================================

BASIC MINDFULNESS MEDITATION:
1. Find quiet space, sit comfortably
2. Set timer for 5-20 minutes
3. Close eyes or soft gaze downward
4. Focus on breath (in and out)
5. When mind wanders, gently return to breath
6. No judgment, just notice and return

Best For: Anxiety, stress, depression, focus
Frequency: Daily practice, start with 5 minutes
Evidence: Reduces anxiety and depression, increases emotional regulation

BODY SCAN MEDITATION:
1. Lie down or sit comfortably
2. Starting at toes, notice sensations
3. Slowly move attention up body
4. Notice tension, warmth, tingling
5. Breathe into areas of tension
6. Complete scan from toes to head (10-20 minutes)

Best For: Physical tension, anxiety, sleep preparation
Evidence: Reduces stress and improves body awareness

LOVING-KINDNESS MEDITATION:
1. Sit comfortably, close eyes
2. Think of someone you love
3. Silently repeat: "May you be happy, may you be healthy, may you be safe"
4. Extend to yourself, then others, then all beings
5. Practice for 10-15 minutes

Best For: Depression, low self-esteem, relationship issues
Evidence: Increases positive emotions and self-compassion

MINDFUL WALKING:
1. Walk slowly, notice each step
2. Feel foot lifting, moving, placing
3. Notice body movement, breath
4. If mind wanders, return to sensations of walking
5. Practice for 10-20 minutes

Best For: Restlessness, anxiety, combines movement with mindfulness

================================================================================
SOCIAL CONNECTION STRATEGIES
================================================================================

FOR DEPRESSION (COMBATING ISOLATION):
- Schedule social activities even when you don't feel like it
- Start small: coffee with one friend
- Join support group (in-person or online)
- Volunteer (helps others, provides purpose)
- Take class or join club based on interest
- Reach out to one person daily (text, call, or visit)

FOR ANXIETY (MANAGING SOCIAL FEARS):
- Practice gradual exposure to social situations
- Start with low-stress interactions
- Prepare conversation topics in advance
- Use grounding techniques before/during social events
- Focus on listening rather than performing
- Remember: most people are focused on themselves, not judging you

MAINTAINING RELATIONSHIPS:
- Regular check-ins with friends/family
- Be honest about struggles (reduces isolation)
- Ask for specific help when needed
- Express gratitude to others
- Set boundaries to protect energy

Evidence: Social connection is protective factor for mental health

================================================================================
JOURNALING TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

GRATITUDE JOURNALING:
- Write 3-5 things you're grateful for daily
- Be specific (not just "family" but "my sister called to check on me")
- Notice small things (warm coffee, sunny day)
- Best time: morning or before bed

Best For: Depression, negative thinking, low mood
Evidence: Increases happiness and life satisfaction

THOUGHT RECORD:
- Situation: What happened?
- Thoughts: What went through your mind?
- Emotions: What did you feel? (Rate 1-10)
- Evidence: Facts supporting/contradicting thought
- Alternative thought: More balanced perspective
- Outcome: Re-rate emotion intensity

Best For: Anxiety, depression, cognitive distortions
Evidence: Core CBT technique

WORRY TIME:
- Schedule 15-20 minutes daily for worrying
- Write all worries during this time
- Outside worry time, postpone worries ("I'll think about this during worry time")
- After worry time, engage in pleasant activity

Best For: Generalized anxiety, rumination
Evidence: Reduces overall worry and anxiety

EMOTION TRACKING:
- Rate mood 1-10 throughout day
- Note activities, sleep, food, social contact
- Identify patterns (what improves/worsens mood)
- Use insights to plan self-care

Best For: Depression, mood swings, identifying triggers

================================================================================
CRISIS MANAGEMENT TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

IMMEDIATE DISTRESS TOLERANCE:
TIPP Skills (DBT):
- Temperature: Splash cold water on face, hold ice
- Intense exercise: Do jumping jacks, run in place
- Paced breathing: Slow, deep breaths
- Progressive muscle relaxation: Tense and release muscles

DISTRACTION TECHNIQUES:
- Engage in absorbing activity (puzzle, game, art)
- Help someone else
- Watch funny videos
- Listen to upbeat music
- Clean or organize something
- Count objects in room

SELF-SOOTHING (5 SENSES):
- Vision: Look at beautiful images, nature
- Hearing: Calming music, nature sounds
- Smell: Essential oils, favorite scent
- Taste: Favorite tea, piece of chocolate
- Touch: Soft blanket, pet an animal, warm bath

WHEN TO SEEK IMMEDIATE HELP:
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
- Feeling unable to keep yourself safe
- Severe panic that won't subside
- Psychotic symptoms
- Substance use to cope with crisis

Crisis Resources:
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text)
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- Emergency: 911
- Go to nearest emergency room

================================================================================
STRESS MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES
================================================================================

TIME MANAGEMENT:
- Prioritize tasks (urgent vs. important)
- Break large tasks into smaller steps
- Use Pomodoro technique (25 min work, 5 min break)
- Schedule breaks and self-care
- Learn to say no to non-essential commitments

PROBLEM-SOLVING:
1. Define the problem clearly
2. Brainstorm possible solutions (no judgment)
3. Evaluate pros/cons of each solution
4. Choose best option
5. Make action plan
6. Implement and evaluate

RELAXATION TECHNIQUES:
- Progressive muscle relaxation
- Guided imagery (visualize peaceful place)
- Listen to calming music
- Take warm bath
- Gentle stretching or yoga

LIFESTYLE FACTORS:
- Balanced nutrition (regular meals, limit sugar/caffeine)
- Adequate hydration
- Limit alcohol
- Reduce screen time
- Spend time in nature
- Maintain hobbies and interests

================================================================================
SELF-COMPASSION PRACTICES
================================================================================

SELF-COMPASSION BREAK:
When struggling, say to yourself:
1. "This is a moment of suffering" (mindfulness)
2. "Suffering is part of life" (common humanity)
3. "May I be kind to myself" (self-kindness)

SELF-COMPASSIONATE SELF-TALK:
Replace: "I'm so stupid for making this mistake"
With: "Everyone makes mistakes. I'm learning and growing"

Replace: "I should be better by now"
With: "I'm doing my best with the resources I have"

TREAT YOURSELF LIKE A FRIEND:
- What would you say to a friend in this situation?
- Would you judge them as harshly?
- Offer yourself the same kindness and understanding

Best For: Depression, low self-esteem, perfectionism, self-criticism
Evidence: Increases resilience and emotional well-being

================================================================================
NOTES ON USING THESE STRATEGIES
================================================================================

PERSONALIZATION:
- Not every technique works for everyone
- Experiment to find what helps you
- Combine techniques for greater benefit
- Adapt techniques to your preferences and needs

CONSISTENCY:
- Regular practice is more effective than occasional use
- Build techniques into daily routine
- Practice when calm to use effectively during crisis

PROFESSIONAL SUPPORT:
- These strategies complement professional treatment
- They are not replacements for therapy or medication
- Work with mental health professional to develop personalized plan
- Seek professional help if symptoms persist or worsen

REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS:
- Coping strategies take practice
- Benefits may not be immediate
- Progress is not linear
- Be patient and compassionate with yourself

Remember: These are tools to support your mental health journey. They work best when combined with professional care, social support, and healthy lifestyle habits.

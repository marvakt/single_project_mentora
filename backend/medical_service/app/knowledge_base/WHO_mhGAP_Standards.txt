WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) - Clinical Standards for Depression and Anxiety
==================================================================================================

SOURCE: World Health Organization (WHO) mhGAP Intervention Guide Version 2.0
EXTERNAL AUTHORITY: This document is based on internationally recognized clinical guidelines.

SECTION 1: DEPRESSION ASSESSMENT CRITERIA
==========================================

Mild Depression:
- Symptoms present for at least 2 weeks
- Depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure
- 2-4 additional symptoms from: sleep disturbance, fatigue, concentration problems, appetite changes
- Minimal functional impairment
- No psychotic features
- RECOMMENDED ACTION: Psychoeducation, self-help strategies, brief psychological interventions

Moderate Depression:
- Symptoms present for at least 2 weeks
- Depressed mood AND loss of interest/pleasure
- 4-6 additional symptoms
- Moderate functional impairment in work, social, or personal activities
- No psychotic features
- RECOMMENDED ACTION: Psychological treatment (CBT, IPT) or antidepressant medication
- SPECIALIST: Mental health professional or trained primary care provider

Severe Depression:
- Symptoms present for at least 2 weeks
- Severe depressed mood AND marked loss of interest
- 6+ symptoms with significant intensity
- Severe functional impairment or inability to function
- May include psychotic features (hallucinations, delusions)
- RECOMMENDED ACTION: Immediate psychiatric referral, combined medication and psychological treatment
- SPECIALIST: Psychiatrist required

SECTION 2: ANXIETY DISORDER ASSESSMENT
=======================================

Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):
- Excessive worry about multiple events/activities
- Present for at least 6 months
- Difficulty controlling worry
- Associated with 3+ of: restlessness, fatigue, concentration problems, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance
- RECOMMENDED ACTION: Psychological interventions (CBT, relaxation techniques)
- SPECIALIST: Counselor or psychologist for mild-moderate; psychiatrist for severe

Panic Disorder:
- Recurrent unexpected panic attacks
- Persistent concern about additional attacks
- Significant behavioral change related to attacks
- RECOMMENDED ACTION: Psychoeducation about panic, breathing techniques, CBT
- SPECIALIST: Mental health professional

SECTION 3: CRISIS INDICATORS (IMMEDIATE REFERRAL REQUIRED)
===========================================================

RED FLAGS - Require Immediate Psychiatric Evaluation:
- Active suicidal ideation with plan or intent
- Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions)
- Severe agitation or violent behavior
- Severe self-neglect or inability to care for basic needs
- Recent suicide attempt
- Command hallucinations

EMERGENCY PROTOCOL:
- Do not leave person alone
- Remove access to means of self-harm
- Contact emergency services or crisis hotline
- Arrange immediate psychiatric evaluation

SECTION 4: EVIDENCE-BASED COPING STRATEGIES
============================================

First-Line Psychological Interventions:
1. Behavioral Activation
   - Schedule pleasant activities daily
   - Gradually increase activity levels
   - Monitor mood-activity relationship

2. Cognitive Restructuring
   - Identify negative automatic thoughts
   - Challenge unhelpful thinking patterns
   - Develop balanced alternative thoughts

3. Problem-Solving Therapy
   - Define specific problems
   - Generate multiple solutions
   - Implement and evaluate chosen solution

4. Relaxation Techniques
   - Progressive muscle relaxation
   - Diaphragmatic breathing (4-7-8 technique)
   - Mindfulness meditation (10-15 minutes daily)

5. Sleep Hygiene
   - Consistent sleep-wake schedule
   - Avoid caffeine 6 hours before bed
   - Create calm sleep environment
   - Limit screen time before bed

SECTION 5: SPECIALIST REFERRAL GUIDELINES
==========================================

When to Refer to Psychiatrist:
- Severe depression or anxiety
- Psychotic features present
- Bipolar disorder suspected
- Treatment-resistant cases (failed 2+ interventions)
- Complex comorbidities
- Medication management needed

When to Refer to Psychologist/Counselor:
- Mild to moderate depression or anxiety
- Patient preference for psychological treatment
- First-line treatment approach
- Ongoing therapy and support needed

When to Refer to Primary Care:
- Physical health concerns contributing to mental health
- Medication monitoring
- Chronic disease management affecting mental health

SECTION 6: TREATMENT PRINCIPLES
================================

Core Principles from WHO mhGAP:
1. Respect human rights and dignity
2. Provide evidence-based care
3. Ensure continuity of care
4. Involve family and caregivers when appropriate
5. Monitor treatment response regularly
6. Adjust treatment based on individual response
7. Address social determinants of mental health

Follow-up Schedule:
- Severe cases: Weekly for first month
- Moderate cases: Bi-weekly for first 2 months
- Mild cases: Monthly check-ins
- All cases: Re-assess at 4-6 weeks

SECTION 7: PSYCHOEDUCATION ESSENTIALS
======================================

Key Messages for Patients:
- Mental health conditions are common and treatable
- Recovery is possible with appropriate support
- Treatment works - both medication and therapy are effective
- Self-care strategies complement professional treatment
- Social support is important for recovery
- Relapse prevention strategies can be learned

Warning Signs of Deterioration:
- Worsening symptoms despite treatment
- New suicidal thoughts
- Increased substance use
- Withdrawal from all activities
- Severe sleep disturbance
- Loss of contact with reality
